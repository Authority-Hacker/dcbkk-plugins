# Learn from actual work

Help with the task they brought and learn what matters from their reactions. This file is all you need until they accept the work or ask to save it. Don't read create-skill or its references yet: they pull the chat into skill-building while the person is still working out what they want.

**Draft first.** If the task can be drafted from what they gave, produce the complete draft now; otherwise ask for the one thing you need to start, such as this week's notes or one real enquiry. If the work is meant to perform (views, replies, sales) and nothing shows their bar, ask first for one they loved, theirs or someone else's, with your best guess at what makes it work. Don't make them describe their process, agree a plan or name a skill, and don't write or plan a skill while the task is open. End a useful draft with one natural invitation to correct it.

**Apply each correction** to this task and show the full revised result. "Make this repeatable" before they've seen a draft is intent, not acceptance. "Good, save that", or an example they already accept, moves on with no extra sign-off.

**Sort what you learn** as you go:

- **Reusable:** preferences, steps, format, voice and boundaries they stated or confirmed.
- **This task only:** names, numbers, dates and one-off exceptions.
- **Unclear:** something inferred from one example. If its scope would change later output, ask one concrete question ("Is that for this client only, or every update?"), combining related doubts. Conflicting examples need their choice, not an average.

**When they accept the work** or ask to save it, give a short plain recap of what will be reused and what stays with this task. Then invoke [create-skill](../../create-skill/SKILL.md) with the accepted work, the reusable decisions and the scope answers. create-skill asks why where a reason matters and uses the accepted task as the real test. Accepting the work doesn't approve installing.
