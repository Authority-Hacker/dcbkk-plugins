# Tidy rules

## Marking the rule list

- **Keep:** one clear rule that still fits the vision.
- **Duplicate:** says the same as another rule. Keep the clearest wording.
- **Stacked:** a narrow case of a broader rule ("no emojis in subject lines", "no emojis in intros"). Merge them into the broader rule, with its reason, when their choices point that way; otherwise ask.
- **Clashing:** two rules can't both hold. The person settles it. Order or dates in the file don't settle it by themselves, though the backup labels help you ask.
- **Fights the vision:** works against what the skill is for. Ask.
- **Changes nothing:** praise words with no object ("be engaging"), a restatement of a complete rule, or an instruction the assistant follows anyway. Cut it.

## Judgment versus slop

Some lines look like padding but carry judgment. Keep their effect, and keep the person's wording where it is their voice.

| Looks like | Actually is | Tell |
|---|---|---|
| Repetitive informal bullets | Steering phrases | Quirky wording shapes decisions |
| Negative nagging | Never-dos | Removing it permits a behavior they hate |
| Optional examples | Voice source | Real excerpts constrain the output |
| A metaphor | A steering principle | It names what good looks like |
| A redundant preference | A calibrated example | It sets specificity, tone or a boundary |

Slop is empty: it constrains nothing. When you can't tell whether a line constrains the output, keep its effect in the rewrite.

## What a tidy leaves alone

Scripts, configuration, anything that spends or sends, and files you can't read are listed, not changed; a change there is improve-skill's job. `.git`, environment files, dependencies, build output and logs are never junk. `.DS_Store`, `Thumbs.db`, `__pycache__` and other cache folders are safe to remove.
