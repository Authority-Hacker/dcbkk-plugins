# The why pass and their examples

## The why pass

On the quick path, use this before drafting, once you know the job and its rules (from accepted work, examples or a brief). When there was no clear standard, use it after the first trial, working from their reaction, with no separate why interview before it. A rule copied from one correction repeats this task; a rule with its reason handles the next one.

Look at what you have: the corrections they made, the choices in their examples, the rules they stated. For each, ask yourself whether knowing the reason would change future output. If not, skip it. Ask at most three questions, one at a time, built from this conversation, never a generic questionnaire, and let them skip ("or I can save it as it is"). Zero questions is fine when the reasons are obvious or don't matter, as with a fixed format.

- **Reason:** "You cut the intro to two lines. Is that because your clients skim on their phones, or was this one just long?"
- **Boundary:** "When would you break that rule?"
- **Quality bar:** "What makes one of these great rather than fine?"

Write each answer into the skill beside the rule it explains ("Keep intros to two lines: clients read on their phones"). If an answer shows the rule was a one-off, leave the rule out. Reasons need open chat; save the host's question tool for a small fixed choice.

## Using their examples

Read across them and name what's consistent: sentence length, tone, recurring phrases, the opening move, the order, what is always left out. Write those as rules and store a couple of short excerpts in an examples file in the skill's references folder. For anything voice-related, ask for examples when in doubt. Never invent one; if none exists, say so and sharpen the questions instead.

## When they say the summary is off

Ask what's wrong in open chat, one question at a time, and update from their own words. Don't squeeze a nuanced correction into preset options.
